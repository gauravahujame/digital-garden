*Zettelkasten* is German for "slip box". It's a note-taking method made famous by the sociologist **Niklas Luhmann**, who kept about 90,000 index cards over his career and credited them with helping him publish roughly 70 books and hundreds of articles.

## How Luhmann's system worked

1. **One idea per card.** Each card held a single thought, written in his own words.
2. **Fixed addresses.** Every card got a unique ID like `21/3d7a`. A card continuing a thought got a branched ID (`21/3d7a` → `21/3d7a1`), so new ideas could be inserted *between* existing ones without re-filing anything.
3. **Explicit links.** Cards referenced other cards by ID, even cards far away in the box.
4. **A sparse index.** The keyword index pointed at only a few entry points per topic. The links did the rest.

> [!quote] Luhmann on his slip box
> He described it as a conversation partner: a system complex enough that it could surprise him with connections he had forgotten making.

## Why it works

The box separates **collecting** from **organizing**. You never have to decide up front where an idea belongs in a hierarchy. You put it next to whatever it relates to most and link it to the rest. Structure emerges from the links over years.

This is also why folders on their own are a weak way to organize knowledge. An idea can live in only one folder but can link to many places.

## Modern descendants

- Obsidian, Logseq, and Roam use `[[wikilinks]]` as the digital version of card IDs.
- [[ideas/Evergreen notes]] restate the same principles for a digital, searchable world.
- This [[ideas/Digital gardens|digital garden]] is a public, browsable slip box.

> [!warning] Common trap
> Spending more time tuning the system (plugins, themes, folder schemes) than writing notes. The method works when you write. Tooling has little to do with it.
